Privacy Policy for Molt

Effective date: [[EFFECTIVE_DATE]]

Molt ("the app") helps you free storage and tidy up your gallery: it finds photos and videos that are likely worth deleting, helps you sort photos into albums and can replace large videos with smaller copies. It is built so that your photos and videos never leave your device. This policy explains what the app processes, what leaves your device, and your rights.

2. What the app processes on your device only

Photo and video library. With your permission (full or limited access), the app reads the photos and videos in your library and their details (such as date, size, dimensions, file type, whether you marked them as favourites and, on Android, the folder they are in). It uses them to find exact and near duplicates, series of similar shots, blurry photos, old screenshots, accidental shots (very dark, blank or heavily shaken photos), photos received in chat apps and large videos, and to offer review by month and by trip. Photos received in WhatsApp or Telegram are recognized on Android by the folders those apps save to, and on iPhone by albums named "WhatsApp" or "Telegram". This analysis runs entirely on your device and also works offline.

iCloud Photos on iPhone (optional, on by default). If you use iCloud Photos with "Optimize iPhone Storage", the original of some photos is stored only in iCloud and your iPhone keeps just a small preview. While you are looking at such a photo (on a swipe card or in the full-screen view) and the setting "Load sharp versions from iCloud" is on (Settings, on by default), iOS downloads that photo from your own iCloud so that it can be shown sharply. The download is made by iOS from Apple's iCloud under your Apple Account, not by the app: the app itself sends nothing anywhere, it does not save the downloaded image, and the scan, the analysis, the recognition and video shrinking never download anything. You can turn the setting off at any time; photos stored only in iCloud are then shown as the small preview on your iPhone.

Recognizing documents, codes and faces (on device). To suggest albums for receipts, invoices, tickets, IDs and other documents, and to warn you before you delete a photo that contains a ticket or a QR code, the app examines photos that look like paper and screenshots. On iPhone it uses Apple's Vision framework built into iOS to read text and codes; on Android it uses its own analysis of the layout of text lines and the open-source ZXing code reader, and it does not read the words. Any recognized text is used only in the device's memory: it is never saved, logged or sent. The app keeps only the kind of item (for example "receipt", "ticket" or "ID document"), a confidence score and which kinds of codes were found. On iPhone only, within a series of similar shots, the app also detects faces to point out closed eyes, a face looking away or a face cut off at the edge. It keeps only the number of faces, these hints and a quality score. It does not recognize who is in a photo, does not create face templates and does not compare faces between photos.

Photo locations for trips. "Trips and events" groups your photos by the time they were taken and, where available, by the place stored in the photo. The app reads photo locations only after you turn on "Use photo locations for trips" (Settings or the Trips screen, off by default); while it is off, trips are found by date only. On iPhone the app then reads the location that iOS stores with each photo, as part of the photo access you granted, while it builds the list; it is kept only in memory and not saved by the app. On Android, Android may ask you to allow access to photo locations when you turn the setting on, and the app then saves a rounded location (to about 1 km) for each photo in its private cache so that it does not have to read the photo again; turning the setting off deletes these saved locations. With the setting on, on both platforms the app works out the area where you took photos on the most days only to label a trip "Away from home"; this is not saved. The app shows no place names and no maps and looks nothing up on the internet.

Albums and moving photos. When you swipe a photo up or accept an album suggestion, the app puts the photo into the album you chose. On iPhone the photo is added to the album and stays in your library as before; the app remembers which albums it created. On Android, albums are folders, so the photo is moved into the folder Pictures/<album> after you confirm the system's dialog; if you back up only some folders (for example in Google Photos), check that the new folder is backed up too. Your chosen albums, the photos waiting for an album and a short record of a move in progress (to finish or undo it safely if the app is interrupted) are stored on your device only.

Temporary photos. If you mark a photo as temporary, the app stores the photo's identifier, its size and the dates of marking and of the reminder (after 7 days), so that it can ask you later whether to delete it.

Videos: shrinking and Live Photos (only when you start it). When you choose to shrink videos, the app makes a smaller copy of each video on your device, saves the copy to your library with the original date and place (on iPhone also in the same albums) and deletes the original only after you confirm the system's dialog. On iPhone you can also turn Live Photos into still photos: the app saves the photo part of the Live Photo, unchanged and with its details, as a new photo and deletes the original Live Photo only after you confirm the system's dialog. While this runs, the copies are kept temporarily in the app's private storage and deleted afterwards. To finish or undo the work safely if the app is interrupted, the app keeps a short record of the job (identifiers of the original and of the copy, the temporary file and the size) until it is done. On Android the app asks for access to photo locations when you first shrink a video, so that the copy keeps the place where the video was taken; if you refuse, the copy has no place but keeps its date. On Android a notification shows the progress while the work continues, and you can play the copy before replacing the original in your device's video player, which receives read-only access to that one temporary file. Nothing is uploaded or downloaded.

Local analysis cache. To avoid analysing the same photo twice, the app stores small numeric results for each photo (an image hash, a mean colour, a sharpness score and brightness figures) together with technical details such as the photo's identifier, size and date, and the results of the recognition and, on Android if you turned it on, the rounded location described above. The cache contains no images, no pixels and no recognized text. It is stored in the app's private storage and is excluded from cloud and device backups.

Your choices and settings. The photos you put in the in-app bin or keep, the number of deletions used today, your language, sound and haptics settings, whether you have seen the introduction, your album and widget settings, your autopilot rules and the last known status of your Pro purchase are stored on your device only. On iPhone, these settings (not the analysis cache) are kept in iOS's standard settings storage and may be included in the backup that iOS makes of your device to iCloud or to your computer, if you use it.

Deletion. Photos and videos are deleted only after you confirm the system's own dialog. On iPhone, deleted items are moved to "Recently Deleted" in the Photos app for 30 days; with iCloud Photos, deletion also applies to your other devices. On Android, items are deleted permanently once you confirm.

Autopilot rules (Pro, all off by default). If you turn on a rule (for example "old screenshots"), the app prepares on your device a batch from its own suggestions that match the rule. It never deletes anything by itself: you review the batch and every deletion still needs your confirmation in the system's dialog. Photos you keep from a batch are remembered so that they are not proposed again.

Notifications (optional, off by default). If you turn on "Remind me to tidy up" in Settings (or "Tell me when a batch is ready" for autopilot), the app may show you a notification that your gallery could use a tidy-up or that a batch is ready. Notifications are created by the app on your device; we use no push notification service and no server sends them. To decide whether a reminder is worthwhile, the app uses its on-device analysis: on Android it re-checks your photo library in the background, at most about once per period you choose (weekly, every two weeks or monthly); on iPhone it uses the result of your last scan, and iOS may occasionally let the app count new photos in the background. For this the app stores on your device only the time of your last visit and of the last reminder, how many reminders you have not yet opened, and the last suggestion counts. None of it is sent anywhere, and a notification contains only these numbers (how many photos, how much space), never a photo. You can turn reminders off in the app's Settings or in your system's notification settings at any time; switching them off stops the background check. On Android, while videos are being shrunk, the app also shows a progress notification.

Progress history and statistics. To show "In total: 14.2 GB freed" and your storage ring, the app keeps on your device a small log of your cleanups: when you deleted, shrank or moved items, how many (and how many of them were videos), and how many bytes were freed. It holds no photo, name, location or photo identifier and is not sent anywhere. The log is kept so that your totals survive a new scan or a change of settings; there is no option in the app to delete it, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app. The storage ring reads only the total and free space of your device, to display them to you.

Home-screen widget (optional, only if you add it). If you add the Molt widget to your home screen, the app prepares five photos for the day from its suggestions and the widget shows small thumbnails of them with a progress count. On Android the app loads the thumbnails from your photo library when the widget is drawn and hands them to your home-screen app to display, as with any widget; it stores no copies. On iPhone the app saves small copies in its private shared container (an App Group that only the app and its widget can read) and deletes them when they are no longer needed or when access to your photos is lost; these copies are excluded from iCloud and computer backups of your iPhone, and they are removed when you uninstall the app. The widget works offline, sends nothing anywhere and only opens the app when you tap it; it never deletes anything. "Show photos in the widget" in Settings is on by default; turn it off to show numbers instead of pictures, and the thumbnails are no longer made.

Progress card and sharing (optional, only when you tap Share). The app can draw a picture of your totals (space freed, number of items cleaned up, since which month, a ring of your storage). It is drawn on your device and contains no photo. When you tap Share, it is saved temporarily in the app's private cache and handed to your system's share sheet, where you choose the recipient; we do not receive it and the app keeps no record of where it went. On iPhone, if you choose "Save Image" in the share sheet, iOS asks your permission for the app to add the picture to your photo library. The temporary file is replaced at the next share and deleted when the app starts.

Technical logs. The app writes performance figures (counts, durations, memory use) to your device's system log. They contain no photo names, identifiers, paths, recognized text or locations and are not sent to us.

None of this data is transmitted to us or to anyone else. We have no server that receives your photos, videos, thumbnails, fingerprints, recognition results, locations, statistics or choices.

3. What leaves your device: purchases

The app is free to use with a daily limit of free deletions. If you buy Molt Pro (a weekly or yearly subscription, or a one-time lifetime purchase), the purchase is processed as follows:

  • Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play) handle the payment under their own terms and privacy policies. We do not receive your payment details, name or e-mail address. On Android, the Google Play Billing library built into the app is part of Google's payment system: it may send technical data to Google under Google's own terms and privacy policy (see https://play.google.com/sdks/details/com-android-billingclient-billing), not to us.
  • RevenueCat, Inc. (USA) is our service provider for managing purchases. The app's built-in RevenueCat software sends to RevenueCat: a random, anonymous app user ID created on your device, the store's purchase receipt or token, the products, prices, currency, store country and transaction dates, and technical information needed to process the request (such as the app version, operating system version and device language). As with any internet connection, RevenueCat's servers also receive your IP address.

Purposes: to confirm and restore your Pro access (performance of the contract with you, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), and to give us aggregated purchase statistics in RevenueCat's dashboard, such as revenue, renewals and conversions (our legitimate interest in running the app, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

The anonymous app user ID is not linked to your name, e-mail or any account, and we do not use it to track you across other apps or websites. RevenueCat processes this data on our behalf under its Data Processing Addendum. RevenueCat is located in the United States; transfers rely on the safeguards in that addendum, such as the EU Standard Contractual Clauses [[CONFIRM_REVENUECAT_DPA]]. RevenueCat's privacy policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy

Retention: RevenueCat keeps purchase records for as long as we use its service, unless we delete them earlier on your request (section 6). Apple and Google keep their records according to their own policies.

Your photos, videos, thumbnails, analysis and recognition results, locations and statistics are never sent to RevenueCat, Apple, Google or us.

4. No ads, no tracking, no analytics on your photos

The app contains no advertising, no advertising identifiers, no tracking and no third-party analytics or crash-reporting SDK. The only analytics are the purchase statistics described in section 3. The app itself needs an internet connection only for purchases; the analysis, the recognition of text, codes and faces, trips and video shrinking all work offline. On iPhone, with the iCloud setting on, iOS (not the app) may also download your own iCloud photos so that it can show them sharply.

5. Children

The app is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children.

6. Deleting your data

  • Data on your device: uninstalling the app deletes its cache, the statistics log, the widget thumbnails, your choices and settings. Your photos and videos remain in your library unless you deleted or replaced them with the app; albums and folders the app created for you stay in your library too. On Android, turning off "Use photo locations for trips" deletes the saved photo locations at once.
  • Purchase data at RevenueCat: e-mail us at [[CONTACT_EMAIL]] and we will delete the RevenueCat records of your anonymous app user ID. Because the ID is anonymous, please include the store's order or transaction number (for example from your Apple or Google Play receipt) so we can find the record. Deleting this data does not cancel a subscription; cancel it in your App Store or Google Play account settings.
  • Payment records kept by Apple or Google are subject to their policies.

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict its processing, to data portability, and to object to processing based on legitimate interests. Contact us at [[CONTACT_EMAIL]]. We answer within one month.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, in particular in the EU country where you live.
